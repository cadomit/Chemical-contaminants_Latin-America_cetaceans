Interpret with caution

This chart places values by publication year, which is not the sampling year, and combines studies that differ in:

  • statistic reported (means or medians versus range maxima);
  • region and population sampled (for example, highly industrialised bays versus open coast);
  • sample origin (stranded or bycaught carcasses versus biopsies of free-ranging animals) and tissue;
  • analytical approach (GC-ECD versus GC-MS, number of PCB congeners summed, lipid determination method).

Apparent changes across studies may therefore reflect methodological and sampling heterogeneity rather than true environmental trends. The within-study trends reported by the authors (table below) offer more reliable evidence.

Search strategy

Searches were conducted in PubMed, SciSpace and Consensus on 18 November 2026. Cetacean terms (dolphin, whale, porpoise, cetacean, Sotalia, Pontoporia, Inia) were combined with contaminant terms (mercury, metals, trace elements, PCB, DDT, organochlorine, PBDE, PFAS, microplastic, PAH, organotin, pharmaceuticals, UV filters, noise, vessel traffic) and with the names of Latin American countries. Additional searches targeted global reviews to provide a comparative context.

Screening and extraction

  • Records were deduplicated by normalised title and DOI. Where a preprint and its peer-reviewed version coexisted, only the latter was retained (for example, Montone et al. 2022, SSRN and Environmental Research).
  • Studies from the northern Gulf of Mexico (United States) were considered outside the regional scope; selected studies from other regions were kept only as a global comparison.
  • All values were transcribed from abstracts. Studies reporting results only in full-text tables are listed without values; their inclusion would require full-text extraction.
  • Metals and metalloids are expressed in µg/g; organic compounds in ng/g (values originally in µg/g were multiplied by 1,000). Basis: ww = wet weight, dw = dry weight, lw = lipid weight; n.s. = not specified in the abstract.
  • Sampling locations are approximate centroids of the areas named in each abstract and were assigned by the reviewer.

Interpretive notes

  • Standard deviation. Dispersion is reported only as a binary indicator (whether the study provides an SD). Standard deviations were not pooled or recomputed across studies, because differences in sample size, tissue and statistic would render such estimates misleading.
  • Central values. In the species synthesis, the mean is the arithmetic mean of study means or medians; it is a descriptive summary and not a weighted meta-analytic estimate.
  • Basis of expression. For the same tissue, dry-weight concentrations are typically three to five times higher than wet-weight concentrations; lipid-normalised values are not comparable with either.
  • Sample origin. Stranded and bycaught animals may differ in age, health and body condition from free-ranging individuals sampled by biopsy, which affects contaminant burdens.
  • Detection limits. Values reported below the detection limit are shown at that limit. "Several species" groups results that the abstract does not disaggregate by species.

Knowledge gaps

  • No study of contaminants in cetaceans from Guyana or Suriname was retrieved. French Guiana is represented by a single plastic-ingestion record (two Guiana dolphins). Venezuela (Lake Maracaibo) and Ecuador (Gulf of Guayaquil; humpback whales breeding offshore) contribute studies whose abstracts do not report concentrations.
  • Most values originate from Brazil, particularly the Southeast, and from two coastal species, the Guiana dolphin and the franciscana. Oceanic odontocetes and mysticetes remain comparatively understudied.
  • PFAS, pharmaceuticals, UV filters, pyrethroids, phthalates and PAHs are each represented by one or very few regional studies. Microplastic assessments in cetaceans have emerged only since 2023.
  • Research on underwater noise has focused chiefly on behavioural and acoustic responses. Measured exposure levels remain scarce, with one passive acoustic monitoring characterisation for the Southwestern Atlantic, and regional licensing and seismic-survey regulations seldom require acoustic impact assessment.
  • Many studies report ranges without means or standard deviations, which constrains quantitative synthesis and precludes a formal meta-analysis on the basis of abstracts alone.
